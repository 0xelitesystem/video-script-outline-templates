# Explainer (concept) outline

**Length:** 8-20 minutes
**Goal:** Viewer leaves understanding [the concept] well enough to use the term correctly

## 0:00 - 0:15 - Hook

[Open with a relatable example or counter-intuitive fact. "[Concept] is why [familiar thing] works the way it does." Or: "Most explanations of [concept] get one thing wrong."]

## 0:15 - 0:45 - Promise + scope

- What you'll explain
- What level of audience this is for (assumes no prior knowledge / assumes basic familiarity / assumes intermediate)
- What you WON'T cover (set boundaries early)

## 0:45 - body - The explanation

Build understanding in layers:

### Layer 1: The everyday analogy

- Real-world thing the viewer already understands that maps to the concept
- Acknowledge the analogy's limits ("the analogy breaks down when...")

### Layer 2: The actual mechanism

- How it really works
- Visual aid (diagram, animation, demo)
- Connect back to the analogy

### Layer 3: Where it shows up

- Concrete examples in software/products/situations the viewer recognizes
- Why understanding this is useful in practice

### Layer 4: Common misconceptions

- The wrong mental models that are popular
- Why they're wrong
- What to think instead

## (~85% mark) - Recap

- The 1-sentence definition
- The 1-paragraph explanation
- The 1 example to remember

## Notes for explainer format

- **Visuals carry explainer videos.** Talking-head explainers underperform animated/illustrated ones for complex concepts.
- **Repeat the key terms.** Viewers learn vocabulary by hearing it 3-4 times in different contexts.
- **Pin a follow-up resource.** Some viewers will want depth beyond the video; link a written explainer or book.
- **Pause for absorption.** A 1-2 second visual pause after a key insight gives viewers time to process. Most explainer scripts are too dense.
